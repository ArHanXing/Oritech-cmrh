---
title: Reactor Heat Recovery Port
id: oritech:reactor_recovery
type: block
---
Heat recovery ports pull heat out of every neighboring component and turn it directly into energy.

Unlike the [heat absorber](@oritech:reactor_condenser), a recovery port needs no coolant at all, and it is not
limited to one component: it drains a fixed amount from **every** heated neighbor, so it is strongest when
wedged between several hot components.

For each neighboring component it removes up to 8 heat per tick, and every point of removed heat is converted
into 2 RF. As with fuel rods, the generated energy is multiplied by the number of layers, while the heat removal
itself is per layer. A recovery port never stores or produces heat of its own, it only moves it.

Because it removes heat rather than simply tolerating it, a recovery port also buys you headroom against meltdown.
The tradeoff is that it removes less heat per neighbor than a heat absorber does, so a reactor that sits close to
its melting point still wants absorbers, while a reactor with spare cooling headroom can profit from recovery ports.

Note that the removal rate has to stay competitive with what the fuel rods produce. Converting heat into energy
earns 2 RF per heat, while a fuel rod at the limit of its pulse count earns roughly 128 / pulses RF per heat,
so recovery ports are meant as a supplement and not as the main power source.

For detailed information on reactor mechanics, see the [fission reactor guide]($reactor/introduction).
